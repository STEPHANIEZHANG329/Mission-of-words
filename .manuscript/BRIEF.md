# Book Brief — A Light on Halloween Night

## Audience
Christian families with children ages 4-8. Designed for read-aloud use, bedtime/fall seasonal reading, church fall-festival gifting, and family discussion.

## Reader problem / emotional need
Halloween imagery can feel exciting and a little scary at the same time. The book gives children a gentle Christian frame for darkness, fear, costumes, pumpkins, and evening celebrations without centering horror or occult themes.

## Story objective
Move the child reader through five emotional beats:
1. Excitement about the fall evening.
2. A small, believable fear when daylight fades.
3. Reassurance that God is near.
4. Concrete acts of kindness and gratitude.
5. A confident ending: we can shine light through love and courage.

## Faith anchors
- Matthew 5:16 — shine your light.
- Isaiah 41:10 — do not fear; God is with you.
- Ephesians 4:32 — be kind to one another.
- Genesis 1:31 — God saw creation and it was very good.
- 1 Thessalonians 5:18 — give thanks.

Use references and original child-friendly paraphrase in the manuscript. Do not bulk quote a modern copyrighted Bible translation.

## Tone
Warm, playful, calm, autumnal, reassuring, joyful. Never grim, threatening, preachy, or supernatural-horror driven.

## Visual strategy for this test
Use recoverable visuals from the prior `Bright Hearts` production artifact as visual source material. Prioritize pages/scenes already containing:
- church and lanterns
- pumpkins and fall trees
- a child on an evening path
- harvest/festival scenes
- children including another child
- creation/nature scene
- table/gratitude scene
- closing children / shining-light motif

Puzzles, labels, mission badges, and activity instructions visible in the old page previews are not part of the new picture-book concept. They are treated as source-layout material only and must not be mistaken for final picture-book art.

## Commercial test hypothesis
A faith-forward Halloween/fall picture book may appeal to Christian parents who want seasonal content without horror emphasis. This Scriveno test is intended to validate whether the existing visual library can support a coherent 48-page story before any new art spend.

## Stop conditions
Stop before final KDP layout. No new image generation, no paid API calls, no final cover/spine/bleed work, and no merge to `main` until the owner reviews the manuscript and image mapping.

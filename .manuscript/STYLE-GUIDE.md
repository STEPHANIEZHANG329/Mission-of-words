# STYLE GUIDE — A Light on Halloween Night

## Voice
- Read-aloud friendly for ages 4-8.
- Warm, calm, concrete, and visual.
- Short sentences; occasional gentle sound words are welcome.
- Avoid abstract theology when a child-sized action can show the idea.
- Faith language should feel natural inside family life, not like a sermon pasted onto the story.

## Sentence and paragraph rhythm
- Usually 1-3 short sentences per page.
- Prefer one strong image or emotional beat per page.
- Use repetition selectively: “Light can be…” and “God is with me” are intentional anchors.
- Keep vocabulary accessible while allowing a few vivid words such as shimmered, twinkled, rustled, and lantern.

## Christian framing
- God is near in fear.
- Jesus/light imagery is positive and reassuring.
- Kindness, gratitude, hospitality, courage, and prayer are the main expressions of faith.
- Use Bible references and original child-friendly paraphrase unless a public-domain translation is intentionally chosen later.

## Halloween / seasonal rules
- Halloween is a setting, not the spiritual center of the book.
- Allowed: pumpkins, lanterns, costumes, leaves, evening darkness, church fall festival, candy/treats in moderation.
- Avoid: occult instruction, summoning, demons, gore, threatening monsters, realistic terror, or glamorizing cruelty.
- Mild “spooky-looking” shadows are acceptable only when resolved into ordinary safe objects.

## Character rules
- Mia: observant, kind, sometimes nervous, learns courage through faith and action.
- Eli: encouraging, curious, active, notices ways to help.
- Adults: calm, present, reassuring; never dismiss the child’s fear.
- New friend: unnamed in first draft so the art can determine presentation later.

## Visual-language alignment
Text should describe only what can plausibly be supported by the recoverable visual library: church, lanterns, pumpkins, fall trees, harvest food, children, festival setting, simple path/porch, creation/nature, table/prayer, and closing children.

Do not write visual details that force new image generation unless clearly marked as a future gap.
